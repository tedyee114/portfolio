WIRE STRANDER

CAPSTONE PROJECT | 6 MONTHS

Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing (WAAM) is an experimental type of metal 3D printing that can be leveraged in alloy creation, particularly High Entropy Alloys (HEA's). If feedstock contains multiple metals, they will be melted together into custom alloys. We built a strander to prepare feedstock for this research purpose.

"A benchtop machine that creates twisted cable feedstock for use with WAAM"

CHALLENGE

WAAM (Wire-Arc Additive Manufacturing) is a process that uses a welding torch on the end of a robotic arm to create parts from weld beads. Using a stranded feedstock created by twisting component metals together, research labs have been able to alloy the metals together during deposition. This is useful for printing with metals that cannot be drawn into wire or be commercially purchased. In particular, the near-equal concentrations in a stranded wire are good for making High Entropy Alloys (HEA’s), rather than traditional alloys that are nearly all one component. My senior capstone group was tasked with building a benchtop machine that could create stranded feedstock compatible with the university's WAAM machine.

SOLUTION

With my team of 5, we got to practice product design, specifically working to interface with the university’s Fronius/ABB WAAM cell’s feeder, tubing, and plasma torch, where the machine is currently used for alloy research.

I also took on the design of an O&M manual, which is available online on my LinkedIn.

PROCESS

Personally, I spearheaded electrical, safety, and UI systems. It was a lot of fun being able to consult with the team about what features we wanted, then allocate enough budget to buy quality components and assemble them in a professional machine rather than just hobby-scale.

ANALYSIS

I was also in charge of alloy analysis procedures, where we looked at the springback that was preventing out early motors from being able to yield the wire into a strand.
